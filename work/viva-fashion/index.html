The Thinking

A positioning problem, not a facelift.

Brief

The product wasn't the problem.

A vibrant style guide for a European womenswear retailer, built to feel like a trusted friend with great taste, not a catalogue. The brand around the product looked generic and didn't signal the quality shoppers were actually receiving.

Strategy

A special find, not a discount.

The audience: women 30 to 65, not fast-fashion shoppers, who want a purchase to feel like a find rather than a discount. That reframed the brief, and even shaped merchandising decisions like bundling, because that feeling is the brand's emotional core.

Creative Direction

Warm, never salesy.

Viva Carmine and Coral Blush carry the warmth, Costa Std brings expressive headlines, and photography leans on lush nature and sun-drenched light, warmer than the clinical product shots typical of this price bracket.

"Style that breathes. Polished without the pressure."
Function Driven Language, Viva Fashion Styleguide
The Takeaway

Viva Fashion needed one thing more than anything else: for the brand to feel as good as the clothes.

Starting from positioning and audience insight instead of screens meant every downstream decision, palette, tone, even how discounts were framed, spoke with the same voice. That's the work of a brand strategist, not just a designer.
