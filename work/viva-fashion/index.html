The thinking.

(A positioning problem, not a facelift)
  1. 01The product wasn’t the problem.

    A vibrant styleguide for a European womenswear retailer. The brand around the product looked generic and didn’t signal the quality shoppers were actually receiving.

    The brief
  2. 02A special find, not a discount.

    The audience: women 30 to 65, not fast-fashion shoppers, who want a purchase to feel like a find. That reframed the brief, and even shaped merchandising decisions like bundling.

    The strategy
  3. 03Warm, never salesy.

    Viva Carmine and Coral Blush carry the warmth, Costa Std brings expressive headlines, and photography leans on lush nature and sun-drenched light, warmer than the clinical shots typical of the price bracket.

    Creative direction
“Style that breathes. Polished without the pressure.”
Function-driven language · Viva Fashion styleguide

Where the brand landed.

(Storefront to swing tag)

The identity carried from the styleguide onto the pieces a customer actually holds: the swing tag, the garment label, the embroidered monogram, and the bag it all leaves the store in.

The systemEvery piece a customer holds, speaking one voice.Six touchpoints · one monogram

The unboxing, considered.

(Packaging)

A shopping bag and mailer box carry the brand’s floral motif, with a warm parting line, Hola Beautiful!, printed straight onto the packaging a customer takes home.

From deck to checkout.

Shopify rebuild→186 products migrated→Klaviyo flows→Foundational SEO

The identity was carried into a full Shopify rebuild: mobile-first product and collection pages, a migrated catalogue of 186 products, Klaviyo email flows and foundational SEO. The brand system became the actual customer experience, from the first scroll to checkout.

Two letters, one gesture.

(The mark)

The Vi monogram carries the brand wherever the full logo won’t fit: embroidered, foiled, woven into labels and printed on the bag.

  1. 01
    The V

    A single calligraphic stroke, thick to thin, that reads more like a hand-signed flourish than a typeset letter.

  2. 02
    The i

    A second, upright stroke that sweeps out at the foot, completing Vi for Viva.

  3. 03
    The shared dot

    The V’s rounded terminal doubles as the dot of the i, so the two letters lean on each other.

  4. 04
    The movement

    Every curve has the swing of fabric in motion: European polish with a Latin soul.
